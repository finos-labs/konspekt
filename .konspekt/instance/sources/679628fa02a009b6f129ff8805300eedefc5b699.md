**User:** Let's think about a roadmap item that'd deal with legal aspect (who is responsible) of GenAI. I think we have enough info, and need to focus on the application

**Claude:** konspekt doesn't answer the legal question of who is liable. Liability is a legal determination that depends on jurisdiction and context. What konspekt produces is the attribution record any responsibility question runs on — a verbatim, tamper-evident, portable answer to "who did what, and who signed off." The law decides responsibility; konspekt makes the underlying facts answerable with evidence. […] Propose→accept is the accountability mechanism. Nothing enters accepted state without a named maintainer flipping review. That maintainer took the decision, so responsibility for accepted content attaches to a named human by construction. […] An AI can propose all day; it never accepts, so it never holds the accountable position. Provenance supplies the evidence. […] Three things the application needs added: Proposer attribution. […] each proposal records which agent and model produced it. An accountability projection. A query that, for any accepted node, returns the full chain: the source excerpt (both sides), the proposer, the acceptor, and the timestamps. […] Non-repudiation, as an open question. […] Signed accepts […] would close that. […] Two boundaries the node has to state or it overclaims. First, konspekt records responsibility for accepted graph state; AI output that never enters the graph […] is outside its record […]. Second, the maintainers list names handles, and binding a handle to a real legal person is identity infrastructure konspekt points at rather than provides.

**User:** 1. Its own goal 2. Optional, unless an instance is configured.. might as well introduce a "legally bound" persona" 3. State the exclusion 4. Explain what you mean

**Claude:** What identity binding means. The maintainers list names handles […]. Legal responsibility needs the link from that handle to a specific real person […]. konspekt records and protects what the handle did; it does not establish that the handle belongs to the person. […] The legally-bound persona. […] Model it as an attribute a maintainer entry carries in project.md. A default maintainer accepts as today […]. A maintainer carrying the legally-bound persona has accepts that must be signed by a key bound to the instance's configured identity authority […]. One abstraction question […]: either personas become the general mechanism for "a role with rules attached," and legally-bound is one such rule, or personas stay for authoring and legal binding is modeled as an accept-mode attribute […].
